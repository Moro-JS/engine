# @morojs/engine 1.1.13

A scheduling release for the epoll transport. Nothing on the wire changes;
no export or capability flag is added, removed or renamed. The default
behaviour is 1.1.12's; one environment variable selects two more service
orders.

## epoll transport: three service orders

1.1.12 serves each loop turn's readable sockets grouped by the CPU their
peer sends from. That order wins wherever the engine answers the request
itself and wherever a peer drives a few connections per thread, and loses
to plain arrival order, by about a quarter at 256 connections, when every
request enters JavaScript under a generator that oversubscribes its cores.
No fixed middle ground exists: shorter runs per group were slower under
both kinds of peer.

`MORO_ENGINE_SERVE_ORDER` now selects the order for the process:

- `grouped` (the default, unchanged): by peer CPU, socket number within a
  group, leading group rotating per turn.
- `arrival`: as the kernel reported them.
- `auto`: the loop measures both and keeps whichever the current peers
  answer faster. A probe is four alternating windows of 200 ms (the first
  50 ms of each, while the peers settle into the new order, not counted; a
  probe whose two windows of one order disagree by more than 30% saw the
  load change and is repeated; leaving the grouped order takes two
  consecutive probes that agree, returning to it takes one). The winner by
  more than 8% then serves for a stay that grows with the margin, from 1 s
  for a near tie to 16 s at a 20% margin, doubling with repeated wins up to
  64 s, on boundaries of the monotonic clock so the loops of one host probe
  in step. A stay ends early when the peers change: the live rate moves a
  quarter away from what the probe measured, or more new connections arrive
  in a second than a quarter of the sockets served per turn.

`auto` is not the default because its benefit is bounded to the one shape
above and, in runs of fifteen seconds, depends on where its first probes
fall; on steady load it settles on the right order within a few seconds and
then costs under one percent. `MORO_ENGINE_SERVE_DEBUG=1` logs every probe
and decision to stderr for anyone tuning it.

## Verified

macOS (libuv) and the Linux lanes (epoll in each of the three orders,
io_uring, Docker's default seccomp profile, musl): 221 suite tests each,
the C++ unit tests, the smoke test, no export drift.

Measured on the recipe of the 1.1.12 note (a two-core server, the
generator on its own cores): the default is within noise of 1.1.12 on both
generators and both route shapes. On the shape that motivated the release,
a JavaScript handler per request under the twelve-thread generator at 256
connections, `arrival` is about a quarter ahead of the default and `auto`
lands between the two depending on where a run's first probes fall; under
the six-thread generator on the same shape the default is a quarter ahead
of `arrival` and `auto` stays within a few percent of the default.
