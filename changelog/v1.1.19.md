# @morojs/engine 1.1.19

An epoll-transport release. Nothing on the wire changes and no export is
added, removed or renamed; what changes is how many system calls a loop makes
when more sockets are ready in one turn than libuv reports in one wait.

## Each ready socket served once per loop turn

The epoll transport records readable sockets in the poll phase and serves
them afterwards, grouped by peer CPU (1.1.12). Its watchers are
level-triggered and nothing is read while recording, so a socket reported
once is reported again by every further `epoll_wait` of the same turn - and
libuv waits again, up to 48 times, whenever a wait fills its 1024-event
buffer. A loop with more ready sockets than that queued each of them once per
wait and then called `recv()` on it that many times, all but the first for
`EAGAIN`, while the kernel re-polled a thousand sockets per wait to report
what was already known.

The turn's sockets are now served as soon as a full buffer's worth has been
recorded, which is when libuv is about to wait again, so the next wait
reports only what is new. A socket already served in a turn is not queued
again in it; level-triggered readiness repeats the report at the next turn
if bytes did arrive. Repeated reports of a socket still waiting for its
service are folded into its one entry.

Measured at one worker on one core with 4096 keep-alive connections:

| load | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| reply sent from a 10 ms timer | 14.7 us CPU/request | 7.1 - 7.4 |
| mixed GET / POST | 13.7 | 10.4 |
| `recvfrom` calls per request, timer load | about 13 | 1 |

Below 1024 ready sockets per loop turn nothing changes: a server spread over
many workers with a few hundred connections each was never affected, a
single worker holding thousands was.

Three regression tests hold 1500 sockets readable at once on one loop:
answered once each over repeated turns, with replies sent later from timers,
and with a second request arriving while the first wave is being served.
