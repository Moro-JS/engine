# @morojs/engine 1.1.18

A request-body release. Nothing on the wire changes and no export is added,
removed or renamed; what changes is how many times a body's bytes are copied
between the socket and the handler, and how much memory an upload holds while
it is arriving.

## Bodies collected where they land

A request JS will see has its head parsed from the read buffer in place, as
engine-answered requests already had; it is no longer copied into the
connection's buffer first. A `Content-Length` body is collected straight into
the body buffer: what arrived with the head is copied once, and every later
read appends to that buffer directly. The body used to accumulate in the
connection's read buffer and be copied out of it whole once complete, so a
10 MB upload held 20 MB at its peak and was copied twice; it now holds 10 MB
and is copied once. Chunked bodies, pipelined follow-ups and every limit and
error path keep their behaviour; seven regression tests pin the paths that
differ from the one-read case.

## Handed to JS without a copy

`getBody()` no longer copies the body into a fresh `ArrayBuffer`. The
collected bytes become the buffer's backing store as they are, freed when JS
lets go of it, and a second call during the same request returns the same
storage rather than a second copy. The storage is released when the request
finishes, so nothing stays pinned to an idle keep-alive connection; a body
the handler kept lives on in JS alone.

The hand-over deliberately uses a plain V8 backing store rather than
`node::Buffer::New` with a free callback. That form books the bytes as
external memory, and a lean handler taking 10 KB bodies at tens of thousands
a second hit the 64 MB external budget every few thousand requests, which V8
answers with a full mark-compact: a tenth of the worker's CPU went to marking.
A note for anyone measuring such a path: V8 frees these buffers in the
scavenge, and a handler that allocates almost nothing on the JS heap never
triggers one, so the buffers are reclaimed by the major collector instead.
That is V8's accounting, not the engine's, and it is unchanged by this
release.
